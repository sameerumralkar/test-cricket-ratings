How a Test is scored

Each team gets match points from three things, multiplied together:

1. Result type. Every result goes in the first category that fits, in this order: win or loss after following on, by an innings, by more than 300 runs or 9–10 wickets, by under 30 runs or 2 wickets or fewer, then everything else. A draw scores by venue; a tie scores the same for both sides.

2. Venue. Credit is worth more away; a loss hurts more at home.

3. Opposition. Before the Test, each side is compared on rating. If the gap is within the band it counts as the same strength; otherwise stronger or weaker. Beating a stronger side earns more, losing to a weaker side costs more.

Rating. Points become a match score of base + scale × points. The rating moves part of the way toward that score after each Test: new = old + α × (score − old). Recent form counts most and old results fade, so no team can bank points forever. Abandoned Tests change nothing.

All Tests to date. The second rating type never forgets: a team's rating is its average match score over every Test it has played, so a 1950 win counts as much as last week's. Each team starts with a cushion of 10 Tests at its starting rating so its first few results don't swing it wildly. Averages sit closer together, so the same-strength band is ±4, and every team stays ranked however long since its last Test.
